Online Order Delivery and Tracking: A Step-by-Step Guide

Learn how to check delivery estimates, enter an accurate address, understand tracking updates and handle delayed or missing parcels.

By Shopora Editorial Team · 8 min read · Updated October 2026

After placing an online order, the next questions are usually simple: when will it arrive, how can it be tracked, and what should you do if the status does not change? The answers depend on the seller, dispatch process, courier and destination. This guide explains a practical way to follow an order without relying on guesswork or sharing personal information with an unverified contact.

1. Read the order confirmation first

Save the confirmation email, order number and any page showing the products, quantities, delivery address and estimated arrival window. Check that the selected colour, size or model is correct. If the confirmation does not arrive, look in spam or promotions folders and sign in through the store’s official website to check the order history, if available.

An order confirmation usually means the store received the order details; it does not always mean the parcel has been dispatched. Look for a separate dispatch notice or tracking number before expecting courier scans.

2. Check the delivery address carefully

Make sure the recipient name, mobile number, house or building number, street, area, city and postal code are accurate where applicable. Add a clear landmark only if it helps the courier find the location. Avoid placing unnecessary sensitive information in public notes. If you notice an error, contact the seller promptly through its published support channel; changes may not be possible after dispatch.

For an international order, confirm the country, region and postal code format, plus any customs information the seller legitimately requires. A missing apartment number or incorrect phone number can cause a delay even when the parcel reaches the destination city.

3. Understand common tracking statuses

  • Order confirmed: The seller has recorded the order; it may not be packed yet.
  • Processing: The seller is preparing the order or checking stock.
  • Label created: Shipment information has been submitted, but the courier may not yet have scanned the parcel.
  • Picked up or in transit: The courier has accepted the parcel or recorded movement through its network.
  • At local facility: The parcel has reached a sorting or delivery facility; it is not necessarily out for delivery.
  • Out for delivery: The courier plans to attempt delivery that day, although timing can vary.
  • Delivery attempted: The courier records an unsuccessful attempt. Check for instructions to reschedule or collect.
  • Delivered: The tracking system records delivery. If you cannot find the parcel, check the delivery location and contact the courier or seller promptly.

Status wording varies by carrier. One quiet day without a new scan does not always mean a parcel is lost, particularly during weekends, public holidays or long-distance transfers.

4. Use the official tracking channel

Open the courier link supplied by the seller or visit the courier’s official website and enter the tracking number there. Be wary of unexpected messages demanding a small fee through an unfamiliar link to release a parcel. If a charge is mentioned, independently contact the carrier using contact details from its official site before paying. Do not provide passwords, one-time codes or full payment-card details in response to a delivery text.

5. What to do when delivery is delayed

Compare the current date with the delivery window in the original confirmation. Check whether tracking explains a delay, weather disruption, customs hold or failed attempt. If the promised window has passed, contact the seller with your order number and latest tracking status. Ask for a clear next step and a realistic update. Keep replies and any promised resolution date.

Include the order reference, date ordered and a short description of the problem, but do not post your address or phone number publicly. Avoid creating duplicate support tickets if one case is already being investigated.

6. If tracking says delivered but nothing arrived

  1. Check the delivery photo or proof of delivery if available.
  2. Look at the normal parcel drop-off point and ask household members or building reception whether they accepted it.
  3. Verify the address recorded on the order.
  4. Contact the courier through its official channel and request delivery details.
  5. Notify the seller promptly if the parcel remains missing, and keep the case reference.

Do not assume a parcel is safe merely because the status says delivered. Equally, do not accuse a courier or neighbour without checking the available evidence. A prompt, documented inquiry makes it easier to investigate.

7. Inspect the parcel when it arrives

Check the outer packaging for obvious damage and confirm that the correct item and quantity arrived. Follow the seller’s stated process if an item is missing, damaged or different from the listing. Take clear photos of the package and product when useful, and keep packaging until you know whether a return or claim is needed. Do not discard materials or use a product that you may need to return before reviewing the relevant conditions.

8. Keep delivery information private

Tracking numbers and order references can expose parts of an order’s journey. Share them only with the seller or carrier when needed, and redact addresses, phone numbers and other personal details from screenshots you post publicly. Verify customer-support accounts from the store’s own contact page before sending information through social media.

A simple delivery checklist

  • Save the order confirmation and expected delivery window.
  • Check the address and phone number as soon as possible.
  • Use the courier’s official tracking page.
  • Contact the seller when the stated delivery window passes or a serious issue appears.
  • Keep photos and case numbers for damaged, missing or incorrect orders.

Delivery systems are not identical, and estimated dates are not always guarantees. Follow the seller’s current delivery and refund policy for the specific order, and keep a written record if you need help resolving a problem.

Remember: Product and delivery policies can change. Confirm the details shown for your specific order.
