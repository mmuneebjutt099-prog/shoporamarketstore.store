How to write a useful page title

A good page title tells people what they will find. It should be accurate, specific, and easy to read.

The page title is one of the first pieces of information people may see when they find a page in search results. It also appears in browser tabs and can be used when someone shares a link. A useful title gives a short description of the page and helps set the right expectation.

Start with the page's main purpose

Before writing a title, finish this sentence: “This page helps the reader…” The answer should describe the page's central purpose, not every related topic it mentions.

For example, a page that explains how to prepare images for a website should not use a broad title such as “Everything About Digital Marketing”. A more useful title would be “How to Prepare Images for a Faster Website”. It tells the reader what the page is about and avoids promising unrelated information.

Make the title specific

Specific titles are easier to understand than vague ones. Words such as “guide”, “checklist”, “comparison”, or “steps” can help when they accurately describe the format. Add the subject that makes the page distinct.

  • Vague: “Helpful Tips for Your Business”
  • Clearer: “A Small Business Checklist for Updating Website Contact Details”
  • Vague: “SEO Guide”
  • Clearer: “How to Review Page Titles and Meta Descriptions”

Keep it natural

Include the main topic in normal language. Repeating the same keyword several times does not make a title more helpful. It can make the wording awkward and reduce trust.

Read the title aloud. If it sounds like a list of search terms rather than a sentence a person would understand, rewrite it.

Match the visible page

A title should describe the content that is actually on the page. Avoid promising a free download, a detailed comparison, or a step-by-step tutorial if the page does not provide it. A mismatch can frustrate visitors and may cause search engines to show a different title.

How long should a title be?

There is no universal character count that guarantees a title will appear in full. Search engines may shorten or rewrite title links depending on the query, device, and page content. Aim for a concise title that communicates the main idea early. Do not add filler just to reach a particular length.

A simple review before publishing

  1. Does the title describe the main purpose of the page?
  2. Can a new visitor understand it without extra context?
  3. Is it different from the titles on other pages of your site?
  4. Does the page deliver what the title promises?
  5. Can any unnecessary words be removed?

Use the search snippet preview to draft a title and description together. The preview is a writing aid, not a prediction of exactly what a search engine will display.

Final thought

The best title is not the one with the most keywords. It is the one that accurately describes the page and helps the right reader decide whether to visit.

Editorial note: This guide is general website guidance. Search systems change, and no title format guarantees rankings or clicks.
